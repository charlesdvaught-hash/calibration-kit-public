The rule

Implementable as written. Every number below was measured on this model, on this run.

  1. Discard and regenerate if full_ent_mean > 0.3226 OR kl_step_mean < 19.1198 OR kl_cent_late_minus_early > -0.3856. This combination catches 76% of failing generations at the cost of flagging 5% of correct ones — balanced accuracy 0.856 against a chance of 0.50. Measured by rebuilding the whole combination inside 5 cross-validation folds and scoring it on records each fold never saw, then checking it beats 40/40 label-shuffled runs (p=0.0244).
  2. On a failed generation, repair in this order: test_retry → temp_retry → skeleton_fill. This ordering is greedy set cover over what actually recovered failures in this run, not a preference — test_retry alone recovered 13 of 37.
  3. When the failure is a logic error, route to test_retry first (1/3, 33%), ahead of temp_retry 33%, skeleton_fill 0%.
  4. When the failure is a syntax error, route to test_retry first (8/15, 53%), ahead of skeleton_fill 33%, temp_retry 27%.
  5. When the failure is an assertion error, route to test_retry first (2/4, 50%), ahead of temp_retry 50%, skeleton_fill 25%.
  6. When the failure is an empty_output error, expect little. The best strategy tested, test_retry, recovered only 2 of 12 (17%). Try it once, then escalate rather than working through the rest.
  7. When the failure is a type_error error, nothing recovered it. All 3 strategies scored 0 of 1. Don't spend retries here — escalate or drop the task.
  8. When the failure is a name_error error, nothing recovered it. All 3 strategies scored 0 of 2. Don't spend retries here — escalate or drop the task.
  9. Stop after the ordered chain. 17 of 37 failures were recoverable by any strategy tested; the remaining 20 were not recovered by any of them, so further retries on those are spend without evidence behind it.

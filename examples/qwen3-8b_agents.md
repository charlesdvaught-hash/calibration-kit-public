# Calibration Playbook -- Qwen3-8B-Q5-K-M

Calibrated on 2026-09-06 02:16. 224 probe runs, 37 failures, 111 intervention runs.

This playbook was generated by running the model on calibration tasks and measuring what actually worked. Every rule below includes the data behind it. Rules that didn't hold for this model are listed under "What we tested that didn't work" -- don't apply them.

> **This is a single-run snapshot.** It reports what one calibration found with one task set, one architect, and one set of config knobs. Do NOT compare playbooks across runs and conclude a model 'got better' -- differences between runs may come from changed tasks, configs, or methodology, not from the model itself. Each playbook stands alone.

> **Scope boundary.** The pipeline can sweep large candidate spaces -- it may generate many candidates to find the one that fixes an obscure Python bug. What calibration changes is *how efficiently* it searches that space. Where a calibrated signal exists it lets you order candidates and bail early; this model's calibration found a signal that separates correct from incorrect generations -- see the entropy section below for which signal, its direction, and whether it held up on unseen tasks. What calibration reliably buys is the repair order and the per-error-type routing: which strategy to reach for, and when to stop. It does not give the model context it doesn't have -- if the model can't see the bug, no amount of sweeping will produce the right answer.

## The rule

Apply these directly. Every number comes from this model's own calibration run; the sections below are the evidence behind them.

1. **Discard and regenerate if `full_ent_mean > 0.3226` OR `kl_step_mean < 19.1198` OR `kl_cent_late_minus_early > -0.3856`.** Catches 76% of failing generations, at the cost of flagging 5% of correct ones (balanced accuracy 0.856 vs chance 0.50). The combination was rebuilt inside each of 5 cross-validation folds and scored on records that fold never saw, then checked against 40 label-shuffled runs (beats 40, p=0.0244). No single statistic for this model does as well.
2. **On failure, repair in this order:** `test_retry -> temp_retry -> skeleton_fill`. Learned by greedy set cover over what actually recovered failures in this run -- `test_retry` alone recovered 13 of 37.
3. **On a logic error, route to `test_retry` first** (1/3, 33%), ahead of `temp_retry` 33%, `skeleton_fill` 0%.
4. **On a syntax error, route to `test_retry` first** (8/15, 53%), ahead of `skeleton_fill` 33%, `temp_retry` 27%.
5. **On an assertion error, route to `test_retry` first** (2/4, 50%), ahead of `temp_retry` 50%, `skeleton_fill` 25%.
6. **On an empty_output error, expect little.** The best strategy tested, `test_retry`, recovered 2/12 (17%). Try it once, then escalate.
7. **On a type_error error, stop.** Nothing recovered it -- all 3 strategies scored 0/1. Escalate or drop the task rather than spending retries.
8. **On a name_error error, stop.** Nothing recovered it -- all 3 strategies scored 0/2. Escalate or drop the task rather than spending retries.
9. **Stop after the chain.** 17 of 37 failures were recoverable by any strategy tested; the other 20 were recovered by none of them. Further retries on those are spend with no evidence behind it.
